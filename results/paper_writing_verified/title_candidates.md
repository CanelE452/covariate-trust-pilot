# Title candidates

Five candidates. None is confirmed; the choice belongs to the user and to the venue.
Every candidate is checked against the frozen guards: no "first", no superiority claim, no
"novel", and no implication that the mechanism replicated on real data.

---

```
T1  When Does Factorizing Intermittent Demand Help?
    A Controlled Study of Temporal Dependence and Its Empirical Boundary
    - carried forward as the strongest family from the outline freeze
    - states the question, the method and the limitation
    - long; the subtitle is the part a venue may cut

T2  Occurrence Structure, Magnitude Persistence, and the Choice Between Direct and
    Factorized Intermittent-Demand Forecasting
    - names the actual asymmetry, which is the sharpest finding
    - no question form; reads as a findings paper
    - risks promising a mechanism the paper labels as association

T3  A Matched Comparison of Direct and Factorized Intermittent-Demand Forecasting
    Under Controlled Temporal Dependence
    - most conservative; describes the design and nothing else
    - safest against every guard
    - least informative about the outcome

T4  Fixed Marginals, Different Answers: Temporal Ordering and Representation Choice
    in Intermittent Demand
    - highest readability; the colon phrase carries the premise
    - "Fixed Marginals" risks reading as the contribution, which it is not
      (the control is excluded from the novelty claim)

T5  When Factorizing Helps, and How Far That Answer Travels
    - shortest; pairs the controlled finding with the transfer boundary
    - omits "intermittent demand", which most venues will want in the title
```

## Recommendation

`T1` for a forecasting or operations venue, `T3` if a reviewer objects that any title
implying a conditional answer overstates a single-backbone study. `T4` should be used only
if "Fixed Marginals" is rephrased, since holding the marginals fixed is an experimental
control and is deliberately excluded from the paper's novelty claim.

## Checked against the guards

```
"first" / "novel" / "state-of-the-art"        absent from all five
implies a universal winner                    none
implies mechanism replication on real data    none; T1 and T5 name the boundary
implies the marginal control is the finding   T4 only, flagged above
```
